Giulio Golinelli
aka giuliocsr — pronounced "giulioceasar", like the emperor

I am a researcher and engineer who likes the awkward intersection of applied cryptography, hardware security, and machine learning — roughly the overlap of three Venn diagrams nobody asked for. I hold an M.Sc. from the Polytechnic of Milan (2024) and a B.Sc. from the University of Bologna (2021); my thesis on the side-channel analysis of SHA-256 somehow won the national DECIFRIS award. And yes, the handle giuliocsr nods to the great emperor of the Roman Empire — who has since retired to his palace in Las Vegas.

I have helped design post-quantum secure processors (RISC-V cores chatting with lattice-signature accelerators), contributed to a post-quantum blockchain ledger that actually shipped — it even has my name on two patents I still find suspicious — and coaxed secrets out of real silicon with an oscilloscope and stubbornness. As a hobby I torment trading agents with distributionally robust reinforcement learning until they hedge against markets more sensibly than I do. I am based in Texas, return to Italy often enough to defend my coffee honor, and have also lived in Singapore, where my spice tolerance learned to outrank my patience for slow compilers.
